What This Paper Covers

Seven Categories of AI in Operations

AI is now running your alert triage, predicting your incidents, executing your remediations, and responding to your customers. Each category introduces failure modes that traditional supportability frameworks were never designed to catch.

01
Alert Triage & Noise Reduction
Suppresses an alert that should have fired. Nobody is paged. The customer notices first.
02
Predictive Incident Detection
Misses a class of incident it was never trained to predict. Support assumes coverage they don't have.
03
Automated Remediation
Makes things worse faster than any human could. No intervention trigger defined. No blast radius limit.
04
Customer-Facing Support AI
Tells a customer their data is safe during an active breach. Confidently. In writing.
05
Automated Post-Incident Analysis
Misidentifies the root cause. The team fixes the wrong thing. The incident recurs.
06
AI-Generated Runbooks
Looks correct. Never tested. Fails during a live incident when a support engineer follows it faithfully.
07
Autonomous Escalation Routing
Consistently underestimates severity for one ticket class. Pattern invisible until hundreds have been wrong-routed.

The Problem

You're Watching Your Systems. Who's Watching the AI?

Organizations have deployed AI to watch their systems but have applied none of the same observability rigor to the AI doing the watching. The irony is precise — the entire premise of Supportability Engineering is that systems must be designed to be observable when they fail. These AI tools are systems. And most of them have never been designed for it.

No observability standard for AI operational tools
You have dashboards for your product. You have no dashboard for your alert triage AI's false negative rate or model drift.
No failure mode inventory
You documented failure modes for your services in the SRD. Nobody has documented the failure modes of the AI responding to incidents in those services.
No intervention triggers defined
Your automated remediation agent has authority to act on your production environment. Nobody has defined when it must stop and ask a human.
No validation for AI-generated runbooks
AI-generated runbooks are never walked through before they go live. The STP step that catches gaps is absent because the runbook was generated automatically.
No accountability model
When automated remediation extends an incident, who is responsible? In most organizations, this question has no answer.
No feedback loop for wrong AI decisions
When your triage AI makes a wrong call, the error is noted and forgotten. It never feeds back into configuration, retraining, or governance.
